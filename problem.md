# The problem

## Who has it
A solo, independent wedding planner who handles every part of the wedding. They plan 10–14 weddings at a time, and each wedding has an average of 14 vendors (caterer, venue, DJ, florist, photographer, officiant, and so on).

When the planner emails vendors about who will handle what on the wedding day, details get missed. Each vendor assumes someone else has a task, or two vendors plan conflicting things for the same moment.

## What it costs them
- **Time:** About 45 minutes per vendor to email them and work out what needs to be covered on the day (from personal experience). With 14 vendors, that is about **10.5 hours per wedding**, or **105–147 hours** across 10–14 active weddings.
- **Mistakes on the day:** When a task is missed or forgotten, the wedding doesn't flow well and the planner is under a lot of stress. Real examples:
  - **No clear owner:** Who moves the guests from cocktail hour to their seats for dinner?
  - **Overlap between vendors:** Who fills the water glasses, the caterer or the venue staff?
  - **Timeline conflict:** The photographer plans to take pictures right after the ceremony, but the couple also needs to sign their marriage paperwork then.

## How they handle it today
The planner keeps a master checklist of tasks, grouped by who will do them. They email each vendor a checklist or survey asking which tasks that vendor handles, then use the answers to fill in the list. They track it all through email, from memory (based on the vendor type and what the couple wants), and sometimes in a spreadsheet.

## Why that falls short
- A task can be missed because nobody confirmed with the other vendors who would do it.
- Some tasks are never assigned at all. Others are claimed by two vendors and nobody notices until the day.
- Each wedding is different, so a list built from memory misses tasks that are unique to that wedding.
- Timing is scattered across emails, so conflicts (like photos vs. paperwork signing) aren't caught ahead of time.
- When something changes (a new vendor, a new dinner time), the planner has to update every email and list by hand.

## How they would know it is solved
1. **Complete task list:** They start from a large standard task list and add a section of tasks unique to this wedding, including suggestions for tasks the planner might not think of.
2. **Clear owners:** Each vendor's checklist or survey answers fill in who is doing what.
3. **Gaps and overlaps flagged:** Any task **no vendor claims**, or that **two vendors both claim**, is flagged for the planner to settle.
4. **Day-of timeline:** A timeline for the day is created automatically. Each task gets a reasonable default time to finish, and the planner can adjust it.
5. **Automatic updates:** When something changes in the app, the task list and timeline update automatically.
