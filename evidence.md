# Evidence

## The problem is real
What I need to show: - **Time:** About 45 minutes per vendor to email them and work out what needs to be covered on the day (from personal experience). With 14 vendors, that is about **10.5 hours per wedding**, or **105–147 hours** across 10–14 active weddings.
- **Mistakes on the day:** When a task is missed or forgotten, the wedding doesn't flow well and the planner is under a lot of stress. Real examples:
  - **No clear owner:** Who moves the guests from cocktail hour to their seats for dinner?
  - **Overlap between vendors:** Who fills the water glasses, the caterer or the venue staff?
  - **Timeline conflict:** The photographer plans to take pictures right after the ceremony, but the couple also needs to sign their marriage paperwork then.

### What research says
- About 2,011,044 US weddings in 2025. ([The Wedding Report](https://wedding.report/index.cfm/action/wedding_statistics/view/market/))
- Couples hire 13 vendors on average (14 in 2022). ([WeddingPro](https://pros.weddingpro.com/blog/entrepreneurship/real-wedding-study-vendor-insights/), [The Knot](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/2023-tk-real-weddings))
- 39% of millennial and 20% of Gen Z couples hire a planner. ([WeddingPro](https://pros.weddingpro.com/blog/entrepreneurship/real-wedding-study-vendor-insights/))
- A planner spends 50–200 hours per couple (one planner's estimate). ([Northwestern Mutual](https://northwesternmutual.com/life-and-money/wedding-planning-guide-10-questions-to-ask-a-wedding-planner))
- Planners juggle multiple events at once; the work is "fast paced and demanding." ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/meeting-convention-and-event-planners.htm))
- "Assuming vendors will coordinate with each other" is a common mistake (opinion, no data). ([Sortra](https://sortra.com/common-wedding-vendor-coordination-mistakes/))
- **No evidence found:** time spent per vendor, how often tasks are missed or double-claimed, or what a missed task costs.

### What people who have it say
Survey results (own survey, n=2, 3 questions):

| Question | Response 1 | Response 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Has a task ever been missed or doubled up? | "Yes, it's easy when you have multiple priorities to forget or miss something, so writing things down and having systems is important. Accountability and learning from misses is critical." | "Yes. When things aren't assigned I've noticed that things either don't get done or too many people work on the same task at once." |
| How long do you spend emailing one vendor? | "Depends on if they answer the first time." Messages once a week unless critical, or sets up monthly calls. Keeps emails "kind, high level," with details on calls. "Always have a follow up email after a call." | "Probably over the span of 2 weeks for a big event." |
| Would a checklist that flags unclaimed tasks be useful? | "A clear project management software, multiple checkpoints and then someone to point out gaps or tradeoffs is critical for success." | "Yes." |

What it tells me:
- **The problem is real (confirmed).** Both said tasks get missed or doubled up. Response 2 described both of the app's main flags in their own words: unassigned tasks "don't get done," or "too many people work on the same task."
- **The flagging checklist is wanted (confirmed).** One said "Yes." The other described exactly what TaskTie does: software, checkpoints, and something that points out gaps.
- **The 45-minutes-per-vendor figure is not confirmed.** Neither gave a minutes number. Vendor communication happens over **weeks** (weekly emails, monthly calls, follow-ups), not in one sitting. The real cost is the ongoing back-and-forth, not one long email.
- **New needs I hadn't planned for:** a steady contact rhythm (weekly check-ins, follow-up email after every call), kind and high-level emails, multiple checkpoints before the wedding, and learning from misses afterward.

Who I sent it to: 2 people who have planned events before, but not weddings. *Limit:* they aren't my target user (solo wedding planners juggling 10–14 weddings), so their answers support the general problem of coordinating vendors on an event, but not wedding-specific details like my 14-vendor or 10–14-wedding numbers. Next step: survey at least one working wedding planner.
What would tell me I am wrong: Planners say mix-ups happen "Rarely" or "Never," or they spend less than 15 minutes per vendor.
**Result:** Not wrong. Both planners said mix-ups happen. Neither reported spending less than 15 minutes per vendor, although neither gave an exact number either.
